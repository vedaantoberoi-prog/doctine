The problem

Health information rarely lives in one place.

Over the years, the details that matter end up scattered. When a doctor asks what you took last time, or when a value started to change, the answer is often somewhere you can't easily reach.

  • Prescriptionson paper, in a drawer or a phone gallery
  • Lab reportsacross emails, chats and different labs' portals
  • Medicinesremembered from the strip, not the prescription
  • Your historysplit between hospitals, clinics and family members' memories

Your health record Available now

One private record of your health, in date order.

Your profile, reports, prescriptions and medicines live together in one account, so the history a doctor asks about is in one place.

  • A health profile with date of birth, height, weight, blood group and allergies, entered by you.
  • A timeline that places each report by the date of the test, not the day you uploaded it.
  • Search by title, type, lab or test name, on your phone or computer.
  • Export or delete everything in your account, at any time, from Settings.

Doctine organises what you add. It doesn't interpret trends, predict conditions or give medical advice.

Documents and prescriptions

Every record shows where its details came from.

A lab value you typed and a value software read from a photo shouldn't look the same. In Doctine, they don't.

How records are labelledIn the app
  • Original only

    The photo or PDF is kept exactly as you added it. Add details by hand whenever you like.

  • Read automatically · not yet checked

    Once automatic reading is switched on, details read by software stay marked until you compare them with the original and confirm. An uncertain medicine name can't be saved until you've checked it.

  • Checked by you

    Values you've confirmed, from a PDF report or after checking a scan. The original stays attached.

  • Upload a PDF or photo, or photograph a page with your phone camera. Available now
  • For text-based PDF lab reports, Doctine finds the values in your browser and you confirm them before saving. Available now
  • Reading handwritten and printed photos automatically. In development

Understanding your information

Plain-language explanations, introduced carefully.

In development · not yet available

We're building an assistant that explains medical terms and the values in your own reports in plain language, points back to the report it used, and suggests questions worth taking to your doctor.

It will only be switched on after its answers have passed our safety evaluation and been reviewed by doctors. When it is, it will use your records only in the chats where you choose to share them, and it will never be a diagnosis or a substitute for medical care.

Automatic reading of scanned photos depends on the same review. Until then, scans are saved as photos and you add the details by hand.

Medicines Available now

Your medicines, exactly as prescribed.

Keep a list of what you take and when, with today's doses in front of you.

  • The dose is stored exactly as you type it. Doctine never calculates, changes or suggests doses.
  • Set the times, every day or on chosen days, with start and end dates.
  • Mark each dose taken or skipped, undo a mistake, and keep a history.
  • Pause a medicine without losing its record.

Reminders appear while Doctine is open in your browser; phone notifications aren't available yet. A reminder is a schedule you set, not medical advice.

Individuals and families

Built for one person today. Designed with families in mind.

Family profiles in development

Today, each Doctine account holds one person's records. Many people look after a parent's or child's health, so family profiles are next. Before they launch, we're settling the rules that matter most:

  • whose account a record belongs to, and who can see it;
  • consent from adults whose records you keep;
  • guardian access for children, and what changes when they turn 18;
  • removing a family member, and taking records with you.

Until then, the simplest way to keep records for someone else is for them to have their own account.

Privacy and responsibility

Your records are yours.

Health information is personal. These are the practices behind Doctine today. Read the privacy policy.

  • Private by default. The database itself only returns your records to your account, and document files are stored in a private folder of your own.
  • Stored in India. Your account, records and documents are kept with our database provider in Mumbai.
  • You decide what's shared. When the assistant is available, report details go to our AI provider only if you tick a box in that chat.
  • No advertising. We don't sell your data or use it for ads.
  • Leave whenever you like. Export everything, or delete your account and its records, from Settings.
